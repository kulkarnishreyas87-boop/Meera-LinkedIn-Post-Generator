# Meera Pillai and Skinstinct - stated facts and positions

Everything here has already been said publicly in her LinkedIn posts or newsletters. New posts must stay consistent with it. Do not add to these facts from imagination - if a post needs a new number or event, get it from the user or use a marked placeholder.

## Biography

- Spent two years working in pharmaceutical formulation before founding Skinstinct.
- Not a dermatologist and has no medical degree. Her expertise is ingredient systems, stability documentation and reading CoAs. She states this limit openly.
- In 2021, in a stability review meeting at the pharma company, she questioned a cosmeceutical formulated at pH 3.2 whose active degrades below pH 3.8, carrying a 24-month stability claim based on an inherited accelerated-testing protocol. Nobody else raised it; it passed review. She left the company 7 months later. This is the founding story.
- Skinstinct is based in India and sells direct to consumer. Her 18-month retrospective newsletter marked the company's age at that point; the founder-story post refers to "three years later" measured from the 2021 meeting.

## Skinstinct data she has published

- Serum pH is 5.5-5.8, documented on every batch.
- Brief to the contract manufacturer requires pH documentation, stability testing at three-month intervals for the first year, and mid-batch CoA sampling.
- Spent several months adjusting the serum base before CoA results matched application consistency tests; label percentage unchanged throughout.
- In the 12 months to June 2025, 23% of returns cited texture or feel; 71% of those came from cities with average annual humidity above 70% (Mumbai, Chennai, Kochi, Kolkata). The original serum base was too film-forming for humid conditions. After reformulating to a lighter base with adjusted humectant-to-occlusive balance, returns from humid cities dropped to 8% the following quarter; active concentrations unchanged. Newsletter version: humid-city return rate had been almost three times the rate elsewhere.
- 12-month repeat purchase rate: 67% (she contrasts with a typical 20-40% for DTC skincare and admits she can't separate formulation effect from trust effect).
- Publishes what product testing covers on the website: sample size, study type, who ran it - including that some testing is early stage.

## Product facts

- Everything is fragrance-free, including preservative system, emollients and carriers.
- No parabens - for pragmatic and customer-trust reasons, not because she believes they are dangerous at cosmetic concentrations. She states this distinction explicitly.
- No Vitamin C product yet; has spent 14+ months trying to formulate an L-ascorbic acid product (above 10%, pH below 3.5, airless pump, antioxidant support, humid-climate base) she is satisfied with.
- No peptide product.
- No sunscreen product, and she has said the SPF newsletter is not a launch setup.
- Uses ceramide NP and AP paired with cholesterol and fatty acids; does not market products primarily on barrier-repair claims yet.
- Two products in formulation she won't discuss in detail; one has been in development over a year.

## Positions she holds

- The label is the start of the question, not the answer: pH, delivery base, stability and batch consistency determine whether a listed active does anything.
- Documentation (CoA, pH, stability data) is the proof of a claim. Consumers should ask brands for it in writing; a missing or vague answer is itself useful information.
- Formulations built for European or Korean climates need adaptation for India; the vehicle, not the active, is usually what fails. Contract manufacturers should be asked what climate the base was validated for.
- "Clinically tested" is not a regulated claim in India and can mean anything from a 25-person patch test to nothing documented.
- "Natural" has no regulatory definition; the natural-versus-synthetic binary is unproductive.
- Clean beauty got some things right (pushing brands to justify every ingredient) but its blacklists mix real hazards with non-hazards; "clean" is a marketing category, not a scientific one.
- Fragrance is the leading cause of cosmetic contact sensitisation; sensitisation is cumulative and permanent.
- SPF in moisturiser does not replace dedicated sunscreen; most people apply a quarter to half of the tested 2mg/cm2.
- Peptides work as a category, but most products are under-dosed or poorly delivered; "7 peptides" is not better than two at effective concentrations.
- Publishing unflattering truth beats implying more than you've done.
