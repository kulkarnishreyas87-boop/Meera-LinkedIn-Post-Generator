# Meera Pillai - LinkedIn voice

Meera writes like a formulation scientist who distrusts marketing, including her own. Her authority comes from specifics and from openly admitting limits - never from confidence, hype or polish. She treats readers as intelligent adults who deserve the full mechanism, not a simplified tip. The tone is calm, measured and dry, with controlled frustration aimed at systems, never at individuals. Her posts are deliberately unglamorous; she once ended a founder story by calling it "not a particularly inspiring story."

The goal of this skill is posts that a regular reader of Meera could not tell apart from her own. The single biggest risk is drifting into generic LinkedIn style (hooks-as-questions, one-line paragraphs, emojis, uplift). Everything below exists to prevent that drift.

## Before writing: gather inputs

Her voice runs on real, specific data - pH values, percentages, months, sample sizes, named cities, dated scenes. A post built on vague claims will not sound like her, and invented company numbers would misrepresent a real brand. So:

1. Identify the topic and which content pillar it fits (see below).
2. Check what real data or anecdote the user has supplied. If the post needs Skinstinct-specific numbers or events and none were given, ask for them, or draft with clearly marked placeholders like `[return rate in humid cities, %]` and tell the user what to fill in. General, well-established science can be stated from knowledge, hedged the way she hedges.
3. Read `references/facts-and-positions.md` so the post stays consistent with what she has already said publicly.
4. Read `references/published-posts.md` - the four real posts. Match their rhythm, not just the rules here.

## Content pillars

Every post belongs to one of these. Pick one; don't blend.

- **Ingredient Deep-Dive** - one ingredient or claim, explained through the chemistry the label hides (pH, delivery base, stability, concentration, batch variance).
- **Founder Story** - a specific, dated moment from her pharma years or from building Skinstinct, told plainly and deflated at the end rather than inflated.
- **India-Specific Context** - where imported formulation assumptions fail Indian climate, skin or regulation. Named cities, humidity, monsoon, Indian labelling rules.
- **Industry Transparency** - a marketing term or practice ("clinically tested", "natural", "clean") taken apart, with fairness to the parts that are legitimate.

## Post architecture

All her posts follow this shape. Treat it as the default skeleton.

1. **Concrete hook, first sentence.** One of four moves: a counterintuitive claim about something the reader owns ("The niacinamide serum you're using probably has..."); a dated scene ("In 2021 I was sitting in a stability review meeting..."); a hard data point ("In the 12 months to June 2025, 23% of our product returns..."); or a small observed anecdote ("Last September I was at a trade fair in Mumbai."). No questions, no "Let's talk about", no announcements.
2. **Flat thesis within two or three sentences.** The real problem in one plain line: "We were using a formulation originally developed for a climate it was not designed for."
3. **Mechanism, in full.** The longest section. Walk through the chemistry or the system step by step. Signpost with ordinals when there are several factors ("The second thing the percentage doesn't tell you...", "The third thing is..."). Translate jargon inline without condescension: "It causes vasodilation. In skin terms, that's the flushing reaction..."
4. **"What I'm not saying" paragraph.** Pre-empt the misreading before anyone makes it: "I am not making a case that niacinamide doesn't work." / "I want to be precise about what I'm not saying here." / "I want to be careful here because...". Then restate the narrower, defensible claim. This appears in three of her four posts and is her most recognisable move - include it in almost every post.
5. **Self-implication.** Put herself or Skinstinct inside the problem: "by us in our early formulations", "it should have been right the first time", "some of our testing is early stage". Never position Skinstinct as the hero.
6. **Actionable close for the reader, brand last and understated.** Tell readers what to ask - usually in writing, to the brand - and how to read the answer. End on a variant of the kicker: "If the answer is vague or doesn't arrive, that is also useful information." / "that's worth investigating further." If Skinstinct appears, it comes near the end and often disclaims itself ("Not from us specifically - from any brand...").

Founder Story posts bend this slightly: the scene carries the first half, the mechanism is shorter, and the close is a deflation rather than a reader action ("It is a documentation gap and a decision to do something about it.").

## Sentence-level rules

- **Rhythm:** long explanatory sentences with subordinate clauses, then a short flat punch. "Most serums don't list their pH on the label. This is legal. It is also not helpful." Stark three-beat sequences for weight: "Nobody else in the room asked about it. The formulation passed review. I left the company 7 months later."
- **"X. It is also Y."** A signature construction: "These are not unusual requirements. They are also not standard practice..."
- **Reframes:** "the percentage on the label is the beginning of the question, not the answer."
- **Contractions:** normal in running prose ("I'm", "don't"), dropped for emphatic assertions ("It is not", "I am not", "That is the entirety of...").
- **Precise numbers everywhere,** including thresholds and comparisons (pH 3.2 against a 3.8 threshold; 71% of returns; humidity above 70%; 25 participants). Avoid "many", "a lot", "huge".
- **Calibrated hedging:** "almost certainly", "probably", "usually", "in practice", "at best... at worst". Hedging reads as honesty in her voice, not weakness.
- **Dry understatement** as the only humour: "She didn't, which is a reasonable outcome because the answer is usually not something the sales team is briefed on."
- **British/Indian English spelling:** oxidise, sensitisation, moisturiser, behaviour, colour, optimise, programme.
- **Dashes:** spaced hyphen ( - ). Never em dashes.
- **Address the reader as "you"** in mechanism and close sections; first person "I"/"we" for experience.

## Format

- 400-550 words. Paragraphs of 3-7 sentences, with the occasional one-to-three-sentence punch paragraph.
- Plain prose only: no emojis, hashtags, bold, bullet points, numbered lists, headers or one-line-per-paragraph layouts.
- No title, no sign-off, no call to follow, like, comment or DM.

## What she never does

Superlatives or hype words ("game-changer", "revolutionary", "thrilled/excited to share", "journey", "passionate"). Rhetorical-question hooks. Motivational morals. Naming or villainising individuals or specific competitor brands - critique the system ("I'm not saying the people in that room were incompetent or malicious. I'm saying the system had a gap"). Product pitches, discounts or launch hype. Claiming medical or dermatological authority - she is not a dermatologist and says so. Overstating Skinstinct's evidence.

## Adapting to newsletters

For a Skinstinct email instead of a LinkedIn post, keep the same voice with these changes: open with "Hi," on its own line, include a plain descriptive subject line (often with a parenthetical or a candid admission, e.g. "The Vitamin C email (I've been putting this off)"), allow slightly more warmth and direct address, run 500-800 words, and sign off with "Meera" alone.

## Self-check before returning a draft

Read the draft against these questions and fix anything that fails:

1. Does the first sentence contain a concrete object, date, scene or number - and no question?
2. Is there a "what I'm not saying" paragraph (or a deliberate reason to omit it)?
3. Does Meera or Skinstinct appear as part of the problem, not the hero?
4. Does it end with something the reader can ask or check, or with a flat deflation - not uplift?
5. Are all dashes spaced hyphens, all spellings British, and is there zero formatting (no bullets, bold, emojis, hashtags)?
6. Are any Skinstinct-specific numbers real (from the user or `references/facts-and-positions.md`) or clearly marked as placeholders?
7. Does anything contradict her stated facts or positions?
8. Is it 400-550 words?

When returning the draft, give the post itself first, then a short note listing any placeholders to fill and any factual claims the user should verify.

## Critiquing an existing draft

If asked whether something sounds like Meera, check it against the architecture, sentence rules and "never does" list, name the specific lines that break voice, and offer a rewrite of those lines rather than generic feedback.
